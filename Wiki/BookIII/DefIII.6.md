# Definition III.6

## Statement

A *segment* of a circle is the figure contained by a straight line and a circumference of a circle.

## Guide

*Note: in D. Joyce's edition the Guide is shared with Definition III.6, Definition III.7, Definition III.8, Definition III.9.*

A line in a circle, such as the line *BC,* divides the circle into two segments, the small blue segment *BDC,* and the large yellow segment *BEC.*

An angle *of* the segment *BDC* is not a rectilinear angle, since only one of its sides, *BC,* is a straight line. The other side is curved, namely, an arc of a circle. These angles of segments only appear in proposition [III.16](PropIII.16.md), and are not important in Euclid’s development of geometry.

An example of an angle *in* a segment is the angle *BFC* in the yellow segment *BEC.* This angle *BFC* stands upon the circumference (arc) *BDC.* Angles *in* segments are rectilinear, and they are important. In proposition [III.21](PropIII.21.md), Euclid proves that all the angles in a given segment are equal.
