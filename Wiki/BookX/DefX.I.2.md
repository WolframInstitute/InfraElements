# Definition X.I.2

## Statement

Straight lines are *commensurable in square* when the squares on them are measured by the same area, and *incommensurable in square* when the squares on them cannot possibly have any area as a common measure.

## Guide

Note that this definition only applies to lines, that is, only lines are ever said to be commensurable in square. Certainly, commensurable lines are also commensurable in square, but lines can be commensurable in square but not commensurable, in other words, commensurable is square only. The most famous example of this phenomenon consists of the side A and the diagonal B of a square. They are commensurable in square since the square on B is twice the square on A, by I.47. But they are not commensurable lines. In modern terms we would say that the square root of 2 is not a rational number.
