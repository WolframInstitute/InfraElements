# Definition I.6

## Statement

The edges of a surface are lines.

## Guide

As in [I.Def.3](DefI.3.md), this statement describes a certain relationship, but this time between surfaces and lines. For example, a hemisphere is a surface, and its edge is the circumference of a circle, a kind of line.

This definition cannot actually be used since there are no postulates to go along with it to connect the edges of a surface in any way to the surface.

Euclid uses the same term for the end of a line in [I.Def.3](DefI.3.md), the edge of a surface in this definition, and the surface of a solid in [XI.Def.2](../BookXI/DefXI.1.md). That term could be translated as “that which is around,” “the limits of,” or “the extremities of,” but in English the terms “the ends of” a line, “the edges of” a surface, and either “the surfaces of” or “the faces of” a solid are fairly standard for different dimensions.
