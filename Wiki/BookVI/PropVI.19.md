# Proposition VI.19

## Statement

Similar triangles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of the corresponding sides.

## Proof

[V.Def.11](../BookV/DefV.11.md)
Let *ABC* and *DEF* be similar triangles having the angle at *B* equal to the angle at *E,* and such that *AB* is to *BC* as *DE* is to *EF,* so that *BC* corresponds to *EF.*

I say that the triangle *ABC* has to the triangle *DEF* a ratio duplicate of that which *BC* has to *EF.*

![java applet or image](propVI19.gif)

[VI.11](PropVI.11.md)
Take a third proportional *BG* to *BC* and *EF* so that *BC* is to *EF* as *EF* is to *BG,* and join *AG.*

[V.16](../BookV/PropV.16.md)
Since *AB* is to *BC* as *DE* is to *EF,* therefore, alternately, *AB* is to *DE* as *BC* is to *EF.*

[V.11](../BookV/PropV.11.md)
But *BC* is to *EF* as *EF* is to *BG,* therefore also *AB* is to *DE* as *EF* is to *BG.*

Therefore in the triangles *ABG* and *DEF* the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional.

[VI.15](PropVI.15.md)
But those triangles which have one angle equal to one angle, and in which the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional, are equal. Therefore the triangle *ABG* equals the triangle *DEF.*

[V.Def.9](../BookV/DefV.8.md)
Now since *BC* is to *EF* as *EF* is to *BG,* and, if three straight lines are proportional, the first has to the third a ratio duplicate of that which it has to the second, therefore *BC* has to *BG* a ratio duplicate of that which *BC* has to *EF.*

[VI.1](PropVI.1.md)

[V.11](../BookV/PropV.11.md)
But *BC* is to *BG* as the triangle *ABC* is to the triangle *ABG,* therefore the triangle *ABC* also has to the triangle *ABG* a ratio duplicate of that which *BC* has to *EF.*

[V.7](../BookV/PropV.7.md)
But the triangle *ABG* equals the triangle *DEF,* therefore the triangle *ABC* also has to the triangle *DEF* a ratio duplicate of that which *BC* has to *EF.*

Therefore, *similar triangles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of the corresponding sides.*

Q.E.D.
